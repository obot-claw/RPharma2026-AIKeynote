# R/Pharma 2026 AI Keynote Outline

Working title: **Agentic AI for Clinical Trial Operations: What Open Source Can Do Next**

Target runtime: ~40 minutes

## Draft thesis

Agentic AI can streamline clinical trial operations and accelerate study data analysis across setup, conduct, closeout, and regulatory submission — but the highest-value path is open, inspectable, pre-competitive infrastructure built by the clinical open-source community.

## Section 1 — Historical context: open source enters the clinical trial pipeline (~10 minutes)

1. **Title** — Agentic AI for Clinical Trial Operations: What Open Source Can Do Next.
2. **Promise of the talk** — Connect clinical trials, open source, and agentic AI into a practical roadmap for R/Pharma.
3. **Clinical operations focus** — Study setup, study conduct, closeout, and submission all depend on faster, trustworthy data analysis.
4. **The old model** — Every company maintained large internal SAS macro libraries and bespoke clinical reporting infrastructure.
5. **The hybrid era** — SAS remains important, while R, Python, and open-source tools become part of the clinical trial pipeline.
6. **Open frameworks with local customization** — Pharmaverse-style ecosystems and company-specific extensions show how shared infrastructure can coexist with internal needs.
7. **Why open source matters here** — Shared tooling makes assumptions visible, lowers duplicated effort, and creates pre-competitive quality infrastructure.
8. **Transition** — Jeremy's open-source projects fit into this wider shift toward transparent clinical operations tooling.

## Section 2 — SafetyGraphics and GSM: pre-competitive clinical monitoring frameworks (~12 minutes)

9. **SafetyGraphics** — Focused on safety monitoring: reusable visual workflows for reviewing clinical safety data.
10. **SafetyGraphics lesson** — Standardized inputs, reusable displays, examples, and documentation make safety review more inspectable.
11. **GSM** — Focused on RBQM and statistical monitoring: broader study monitoring and quality workflows.
12. **GSM lesson** — Monitoring outputs should be generated, versioned, reviewed, and improved like software.
13. **The shared pattern** — Open, shareable, pre-competitive frameworks for clinical trial review and monitoring.
14. **What this enabled** — More transparent workflows, reusable examples, public documentation, and community feedback loops.
15. **Transition** — Once the workflow is open and software-shaped, agents have a surface they can help maintain and extend.

## Section 3 — From code assist to agentic engineering for clinical trial work (~15 minutes)

16. **LLMs became capable enough to change the workflow** — The last few years moved AI from autocomplete and code assist toward bounded agentic work.
17. **Agentic engineering definition** — Humans set direction; agents execute bounded loops with requirements, tests, demos, PRs, and evidence.
18. **Clinical trial operations opportunities** — Setup, conduct, closeout, and submission workflows all contain repeatable analysis, documentation, and validation tasks.
19. **Concrete open-source loop** — Issue → requirements → implementation → tests → rendered demo → PR → human review.
20. **What agents can help with now** — Drafting specs, scaffolding packages, updating docs, creating demos, running checks, summarizing evidence.
21. **What still needs humans** — Scientific judgment, regulatory accountability, risk decisions, requirements, and approval boundaries.
22. **obot demo thread** — A dedicated open-source development agent using GitHub, browser QA, memory, skills, and Telegram feedback to support clinical software work.
23. **Guardrails for clinical contexts** — Auditable actions, permission boundaries, reproducible evidence, validation traces, and no unsupported claims.

25. **Call to action** — Build open, auditable agentic systems where AI workers amplify expert clinical and statistical judgment.
26. **Q&A / links** — Repos, demos, contact information, and next-step community invitations.
